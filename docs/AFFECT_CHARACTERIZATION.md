## 3a. Relaxation alone

Zero drive input, non-neutral start (emotion=(-0.6, 0.6), mood=(-0.2, 0.1), accum_rate=1, emotion_decay=2, mood_drift=0.2), over a 3600 s span, at four tick granularities.

| rate | dt (s) | steps | emotion.valence | emotion.arousal | mood.valence | mood.arousal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| dt=0.1 | 0.1 | 36000 | -0.239392 | 0.14924 | -0.239392 | 0.14924 |
| dt=2 | 2 | 1800 | -0.300561 | 0.225701 | -0.300561 | 0.225701 |
| dt=60 | 60 | 60 | -0.599926 | 0.599908 | -0.200074 | 0.100092 |
| single tick (dt=3600) | 3600 | 1 | -0.2 | 0.1 | -0.6 | 0.6 |

Max spread across the four rates, per axis: emotion.valence=0.4, emotion.arousal=0.5, mood.valence=0.4, mood.arousal=0.5.

**The four do NOT agree.** Each `emotion_relax`/`mood_relax` step is the
exact solution for *that one variable* decaying toward the *other
variable's pre-step value* — a first-order (Gauss-Seidel-style) operator
split of a genuinely coupled linear system, not a closed-form solution of
the coupled system itself. The split is only exact in the dt -> 0 limit;
at finite dt the discrete map does not conserve the same linear invariant
as the continuous ODE (`emotion_decay*mood + mood_drift*emotion` is exactly
conserved by the continuous system's decay terms, not by the discrete
step at dt=2.0 or dt=60.0). So "closed-form and time-invariant" holds
per relaxation term in isolation, not for the coupled emotion/mood pair.

Step-by-step at dt=60 (`emotion_decay=2.0` implies relax fraction
`1-e^(-2*60)` ~= 1.0; `mood_drift=0.2` implies `1-e^(-0.2*60)` ~= 0.9999938 —
both variables jump *almost fully* to the other's old value every step):

| step | t (s) | emotion.valence | mood.valence |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | -0.6 | -0.2 |
| 1 | 60 | -0.2 | -0.599998 |
| 2 | 120 | -0.599998 | -0.200002 |
| 3 | 180 | -0.200002 | -0.599995 |
| 4 | 240 | -0.599995 | -0.200005 |
| 5 | 300 | -0.200005 | -0.599993 |
| 6 | 360 | -0.599993 | -0.200007 |

At this dt the pair does not relax monotonically toward each other at all —
it nearly swaps values every tick (`emotion_v` at step 1 lands within 1e-6 of
`mood_v` at step 0, and vice versa) and only very slowly bleeds toward a common
value across many swaps. This is why the dt=60 row in the table above
(-0.599926) sits almost exactly on the *start* value (-0.6) after a full hour:
the trajectory is oscillating, not converging, at this granularity.

**Bounded:** no. Pressure accumulates without a ceiling as long as the
engine is idle; nothing in `BoredomDrive.update` caps it.

**Tick-rate dependent:** no, not on its own. `idle_rate * dt` summed over any
partition of a span equals `idle_rate * span` exactly, because the rate has
no dependence on the drive's own state (no feedback for Euler's error to act
on). Every rate in the table above lands on `idle_rate*span` to float
precision.

## Measured timescales

Empirical tau (time for `|value - equilibrium|` to first fall to 1/e of its
t=0 value) from the 3600 s relaxation-alone run (3a's start state),
per tick rate, against the declared `1/emotion_decay` and `1/mood_drift`:

Declared: tau_emotion = 1/2 = 0.5 s; tau_mood = 1/0.2 = 5 s.

| rate (dt) | equilibrium.valence | measured tau_emotion (s) | measured tau_mood (s) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.1 | -0.239392 | 0.5 | 0.5 |
| 2 | -0.300561 | 2 | 2 |
| 60 | -0.599926 | 2280 | 2280 |

Temperament, over 36000 ticks at dt=0.1 across the same
span: delta(valence)=0, delta(arousal)=0, delta(control)=0.
Unchanged. `update()` never writes `_accum_rate`/`_emotion_decay`/`_mood_drift`;
only `AffectEngine.__init__`/`from_dict` set them. So temperament is not a
third *timescale* in the running system at all — it is a fixed parameter set
once at construction (or restore) and never advanced by `tick()`. "Three
timescales" as currently measured is two dynamic ones (emotion, mood) plus
one static one (temperament), not three rates of the same kind.

The measured tau_emotion and tau_mood track the declared values closely at
dt=0.1 (the near-continuum rate) and diverge at dt=2.0 and dt=60.0 — the same
coupling/operator-splitting effect noted in 3a: each `*_relax` fraction is
exact for its own variable in isolation, but the pair's joint relaxation is
only close to the continuous coupled solution when dt is small relative to
both `1/emotion_decay` and `1/mood_drift`.

Caveat on resolution: at dt=2.0 the reported tau (2 s) is the *first sampled*
point past the 1/e threshold — a single step — so it cannot distinguish "the
true crossing happened within that one 2 s step" from "coarse dt itself moved
the state past threshold in one jump"; the grid cannot resolve finer than dt.
At dt=60.0 the reported tau (2280 s = 38 steps) is not a single-step artifact —
it is 38 grid points in, consistent with the swap/oscillation trace above
delaying convergence rather than a sampling limit.
